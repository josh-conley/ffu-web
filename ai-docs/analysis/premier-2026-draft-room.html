The rule change that moves every board

Before a single tendency: the thing they were all drafting for has changed.

Defense is gone. A third flex takes its place.

2025 QBRBRBWRWRTEFLEXFLEXDEF6 BN 2026 QBRBRBWRWRTEFLEXFLEXFLEX6 BN

Two effects, both pushing the same way. Every manager gets back the pick they used to spend on a defense, and every manager now has a ninth starting spot only a back, receiver or tight end can fill. Nine of the twelve took their defense in round 13 or later — but Johnkshire and Circle City spent a round 11 or 12 on one. Expect running backs and receivers to run about a round deeper than the boards below suggest, and the late rounds to be pure upside.

Who pays up, who waits

Each manager's average pick against where the rest of the FFU took that same player the same year. The typical FFU manager-season lands within about four points of zero, so anything past ±6 is a habit, not noise.

And who's actually any good at it

Two different questions, and the gap between them is the most useful thing on this page. Judgment asks whether the players they picked in rounds 1–10 produced for anyone. Return asks how much of that production they personally started. A manager can be right about players and still bank none of it.

    How this was built

    Source: this repo's migrated draft and lineup data for 2023–25 across all three tiers — 1,620 draft picks and every weekly starting lineup — plus the 2026 Premier league and draft settings read live from Sleeper on 25 Aug 2026. Managers are resolved by ffuId, so a Masters or National draft counts the same as a Premier one.

    Versus market is a leave-one-out average: each pick compared to the mean overall pick of that same player in the other two FFU leagues that year. Only players drafted in all three leagues qualify. It measures position against this league's own consensus, not a national board — a player the whole FFU under-rates won't show up here as a bargain.

    Judgment and Return both compare a pick's fantasy production to the median for its round across all 36 manager-seasons, then re-centre on this room's twelve. Judgment counts the points the drafted player scored in that league for whoever started him; Return counts only points scored in the drafter's own starting lineup. The gap is trade and waiver activity — a manager who flips picks for value will always look worse on Return, and that is the intended reading, not a flaw. Neither measure knows about injuries, and neither credits what a manager did after the draft.

    Two known gaps: the 2023 Premier and Masters draft files are each missing one manager's draft slot (Team Dogecoin and Dmandre161 — neither is in the 2026 Premier field), so 2023 trades involving those slots are inferred rather than recorded. Defenses are excluded from every positional and value figure.
