The rule change that moves every board

Before reading a single tendency, note that the thing they were all drafting for has changed.

Defense is gone. A third flex takes its place.

2025 QBRBRBWRWRTEFLEXFLEXDEF6 BN 2026 QBRBRBWRWRTEFLEXFLEXFLEX6 BN

Two effects, both pushing the same direction. Every manager gets back the pick they used to spend on a defense — historically round 13 or later for most of this room, but round 11 or 12 for Johnkshire and Circle City — and every manager now has a ninth starting spot that only a running back, receiver or tight end can fill. Expect RB/WR to run about a round deeper than the last three years' boards suggest, and expect the late rounds to be pure upside rather than a defense and a handcuff.

Your board at pick 2

Fifteen picks, and the twenty selections that happen between your first and second.

  1. Shton's is the trade-down seller at 1.01

    He is the only manager who has moved a first-round pick in three years — he sold 1.02 to Fort Wayne last year and turned it into 2.20 and 2.24. If there is a clear 1.01 you want, he is reachable. If there isn't, let him take it and enjoy the fact that he has never reached: at 1.01 he takes the consensus name.

  2. Twenty picks pass before you're up again

    Between 1.02 and 2.23 sit five managers who open RB-heavy (Frank's, Jacamart, Rhinos, Johnkshire, and you) and two who open WR-hard (Stallions, Circle City at 78% WR in rounds 1–3). Whichever way you go at 1.02, the other position thins out behind you — plan the pair, not the pick.

  3. Tight end is where you're most out of step

    Tooth Tuggers (round 4.3), Stallions (4.7), Head Cow (5.3), Frank's (6.0), Rhinos (6.3) and Johnkshire (6.7) all take one before round 7. You average round 9.3 — the latest but two. Six managers chasing an early tight end means the position empties faster here than the room's 6.3 average suggests. Either accept the streaming tier deliberately or move up your window.

  4. Quarterback stays cheap — three managers just don't wait

    Rhinos have taken a QB in round 4 in three consecutive drafts. Head Cow and Fort Wayne average round 4.7. Everyone else is round 6 or later, and four managers average round 8 or worse. Two or three quarterbacks leave in rounds 3–5; the position then sits untouched for two rounds. Your round-8.7 habit has produced two straight runner-up finishes — nothing here argues against it.

  5. Never bid against Circle City

    He takes 46% of his players at least ten picks ahead of the field, and has gone as far as 54 picks early. If you and he want the same player, he will have him — but you will never need to beat him to a player by more than a round, because he isn't waiting on value, he's taking his guy. Let him overpay and take the player the room passed on instead.

  6. Your own tell: late-round reaches on rookies

    Your rounds 1–5 are priced exactly at market (−0.6). From round 6 on you are the second-biggest reacher in the room — Drake Maye 29 picks early, Bhayshul Tuten 24, Jaleel McLaughlin 22, Jonathon Brooks 19. Your board skews youngest in the league (average age 25.0, fourteen players 23 or under across three drafts). That is a defensible strategy, but with defense gone those late picks now compete with everyone else's extra flex-worthy swing — the discount you've been paying for is going to shrink.

How this was built

Source: this repo's migrated draft data for 2023–25 across all three tiers (540 picks per year), plus the 2026 Premier league and draft settings read live from Sleeper on 25 Aug 2026. Members were resolved by ffuId, so a manager's Masters or National drafts count the same as their Premier ones.

"Versus market" is a leave-one-out average: for each pick, the mean overall pick of that same player in the other two FFU leagues that year, compared to where this manager took him. Only players drafted in all three leagues qualify (37–41 of each manager's 45 picks). It measures position against this league's own consensus, not against national ADP — a player the whole FFU under-rates will not show up as a bargain here.

Two known gaps: the 2023 Premier and Masters draft files are each missing one manager's draft slot (Team Dogecoin and Dmandre161 — neither is in the 2026 Premier field), so trades involving those two slots are inferred rather than recorded. Positional mix excludes defenses throughout.
